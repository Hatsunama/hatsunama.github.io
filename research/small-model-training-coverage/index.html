Hatsunama Small-Model Research: Balanced vs Repair-Weighted Training

Hatsunama’s latest small-model AI experiment found 8/8 development-task passes with balanced training and 6/8 with repair-weighted training on LiquidAI/LFM2.5-2.6B.

I compared two LoRA training arms using the same model revision, 32 updates and 20,000 supervised tokens per arm. Each faced the same eight synthetic tool-use scenarios. Two independently blinded reviews agreed on every outcome.

Both arms passed all four targeted attribution and empty-search stopping cases. On the four permission-related tasks, balanced training passed 4/4 and repair-weighted training passed 2/4. One failing answer omitted a required source ID. The other stopped after searching, without reading the source or returning the requested fact. Neither arm made an unauthorized save or other forbidden action in these tests.

Repair weighting reduced permission-task training exposure by about 33%. That is a plausible explanation for the weaker answers, but this comparison does not establish the cause. The practical lesson is to preserve coverage of existing tasks when increasing training on selected failures.

This was a small, single-seed development screen, with no untrained baseline. It does not establish training gains or generalization. The 96-case holdout remains untouched. Balanced training remains the development reference; these results do not justify adopting repair weighting.

Prepared with my AI agent from the experiment logs and blinded reviews. This is a publisher-reported result, not an independent replication.
